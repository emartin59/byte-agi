## 1. Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) & Emergent Complexity

MARL extends single-agent Reinforcement Learning into domains where multiple agents interact, generating complex, non-stationary dynamics. This is a crucial stepping stone toward AGI, as human-level intelligence evolved in highly social, multi-agent environments.

* **Stochastic Games (SG) / Markov Games:** The multi-agent extension $\langle S, A_1 \dots A_n, R_1 \dots R_n, f, \gamma angle$. The state transition and rewards depend on the joint action $a \in A_1 	imes \dots 	imes A_n$.

### 1.2 Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE)
To mitigate the non-stationarity of concurrent learning, CTDE algorithms utilize global state information during training (centralized critic) while restricting agents to local observations during execution (decentralized actors).
* **The IGM (Individual-Global-Max) Principle:** For effective CTDE, the joint greedy action must be equivalent to the collection of individual greedy actions: $rg\max_u Q_{tot}(	au, u) = egin{pmatrix} rg\max_{u_1} Q_1(	au_1, u_1) \ \dots \ rg\max_{u_n} Q_n(	au_n, u_n) \end{pmatrix}$.
* **Value-Decomposition Networks (VDN):** Factorizes the joint action-value function as a simple sum of individual utilities: $Q_{tot}(s,u) = \sum Q_a(s,u_a)$.
* **QMIX:** State-of-the-art value decomposition. Factors $Q_{tot}$ into a monotonic, non-linear function of individual utilities using a mixing network. It enforces monotonicity via non-negative weights generated by hypernetworks: $rac{\partial f_s}{\partial Q_a} \ge 0$.
* **QTRAN / QPLEX:** Address tasks that satisfy IGM but do not conform to QMIX's strict monotonic decomposition constraints.

* **Scalability:** Communication overhead and computational constraints limit large-scale MARL. **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)** are increasingly used to aggregate local information effectively, maintaining scalability while coordinating actions.

### 1.4 Emergent Communication & Stigmergy
* **Cheap Talk vs. Stigmergy:** Most emergent communication research focuses on "cheap talk" (transient discrete tokens broadcast per tick). Advanced research emphasizes **Stigmergic Communication**—using the environment as a persistent shared memory (e.g., writing to a grid). This mirrors biological systems (e.g., ant pheromones) and allows multi-step compositional reasoning.
* **Information Asymmetry:** Cooperative tasks where agents have asymmetric information (e.g., referential games, Lewis games) naturally force the induction of compositional language.
* **Metrics for Emergent Language:** * *Topographic Similarity:* Measures the correlation between message distances (e.g., edit distance) and input vector distances.
    * *Ease of Learning:* Measures how quickly a new "listener" agent can learn the emergent language produced by a trained "speaker".
    * *Channel Ablation:* Causal proof of communication. Evaluate fitness under `NORMAL` vs. `ERASE_READ` (agents cannot see writing) vs. `NO_PERSISTENCE` (writing vanishes after one tick). Drops in performance quantitatively prove reliance on communication.

## 2. Transfer Learning, Adaptation & Optimization

AGI requires agents to rapidly adapt to new tasks, changing dynamics, and rule perturbations without catastrophic forgetting or relearning from scratch.

### 2.1 The "Warm-Start" Generalization Gap & Shrink-Perturb
Traditionally, initializing a neural network with weights from a previously trained model (warm-starting) to train on new/perturbed data resulted in *worse* long-term generalization than cold-starting (random initialization).
* **The Shrink-Perturb Trick:** Solves the warm-start generalization gap. When transferring to a new task, shrink the existing weights toward zero by a factor $\lambda$ and add small Gaussian noise $\sigma$:
    $	heta_{new} = \lambda 	heta_{old} + \sigma 	heta_{random}$ (where $0 < \lambda < 1$).
* **Results:** Significantly reduces convergence time (up to 97% reduction in MARL parameter adaptation) while maintaining or exceeding the test accuracy of cold-started models. It balances preserving useful feature representations with escaping local optima.

### 2.2 Successor Features: Generalised Policy Evaluation (GPE) & Improvement (GPI)
Successor Features offer a middle-ground between Model-Free (fast, inflexible) and Model-Based (slow, computationally heavy) RL.
* **Concept:** Decouple the dynamics of the environment from the reward function. Represent the expected discounted sum of features $\psi^\pi(s,a)$. The value function is then $Q^\pi(s,a) = \psi^\pi(s,a) \cdot w$, where $w$ represents the agent's current preferences (reward weights).
* **GPE (Generalised Policy Evaluation):** Instantly evaluate a set of previously learned policies under a *new* reward weighting $w$ without retraining.
* **GPI (Generalised Policy Improvement):** Synthesize a novel policy zero-shot by acting greedily over the evaluated $Q$-values of all known policies: $\pi_{new}(s) = rg\max_a \max_i Q^{\pi_i}(s,a)$. This allows for compositional generalization to unseen tasks.

### 2.3 Evolutionary Strategies (ES) & Adaptive Noise Controllers
While Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is the dominant MARL algorithm, ES offers unique exploration dynamics highly suited for extreme hardware acceleration (e.g., JAX).
* **Adaptive Noise / Ratchet Mechanism:** In ES, the exploration/exploitation trade-off is governed by the perturbation standard deviation ($\sigma$).
    * Track fitness improvement via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) and population variance.
    * *Shrink* $\sigma$ to exploit when SNR is high; *Grow* $\sigma$ to explore when stalled.
    * **The Ratchet:** If smoothed fitness drops substantially (e.g., >30%) below the best-ever fitness, high $\sigma$ is creating a feedback trap (noise destroys good policies -> looks like stall -> adds more noise). The Ratchet overrides the logic, pulling $\sigma$ down toward the historical `best_sigma` to recover the policy.

## 3. Large Language Models (LLMs) & Agentic Architectures

Modern LLMs are transitioning from passive chat interfaces to active, long-horizon agents.

### 3.1 Long-Horizon Optimization (GLM-5.1 paradigm)
* **The Plateau Problem:** Standard LLM agents exhaust their repertoire early on tasks, applying familiar techniques and plateauing. Giving them more time/tokens yields no performance gain.
* **Staircase Optimization:** Advanced agentic models (like GLM-5.1) exhibit a "staircase" pattern over hundreds of iterations (e.g., 600+ turns, 6000+ tool calls). They undergo periods of incremental tuning, punctuated by structural/architectural shifts when they autonomously identify local optima.
* **Self-Evaluation in Open-Ended Tasks:** For tasks lacking numeric metrics (e.g., "Build a Linux desktop in a browser"), the model must autonomously review its own execution trace, identify missing features or broken UI elements, and loop iteratively. 

### 3.2 Constitutional AI for Agentic Tool-Use (Nanocode)
Training high-capability agentic LLMs on consumer-grade hardware (TPUs/GPUs) using the Constitutional AI pipeline:
1.  **Synthetic Data Generation (Generate -> Critique -> Revise):** * *Generator* produces an initial response/tool-call.
    * *Critique Model* judges the response against a `SOUL.md` (persona/constraints). If it fails, it provides a specific critique.
    * *Revision:* Generator fixes the output based on critique.
2.  **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Train the base model on the finalized, successful (Prompt, Chosen) pairs to align formatting and basic tool syntax (e.g., `<tool_call_start>Bash<tool_arg>command...`).
3.  **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Use the (Chosen, Rejected) pairs from the Critique loop to optimize the model via binary classification, penalizing log-probs of dis-preferred actions and reinforcing aligned actions without needing an explicit Reward Model (RLHF).

### 3.3 Micro-Scale Models & Extreme Constraints (GuppyLM)
* **Parameter Scale:** < 10 Million parameters (e.g., 8.7M).
* **Ablating Complexity:** Removing RoPE, GQA, and SwiGLU in favor of vanilla transformers with learned positional embeddings. 
* **Baking Persona into Weights:** Micro-models lack the capacity for zero-shot instruction following via System Prompts. Instead, the entire training corpus is synthetically templated to embody the persona (e.g., a fish), saving context window tokens and eliminating the need for complex alignment.
* **Quantization:** `uint8` ONNX export enables ~10MB deployments for local, zero-install WebAssembly (WASM) execution at the extreme edge.

## 4. Hardware Acceleration & Compute Asymmetry

### 4.1 JAX and Pure-Byte Environments
* **Compute Asymmetry:** Independent researchers and smaller labs cannot compete with hyperscalers on total FLOPs. The competitive edge lies in **Algorithmic Compression**—stripping away visual/3D overhead (e.g., MuJoCo, XLand) to run massive populations of tiny agents in purely integer-based "byte grids".
* **Hardware Unrolling:** Libraries like **JaxMARL** allow the entire environment step, reward calculation, and policy update to be JIT-compiled into a single XLA graph. This enables millions of environment steps per second on a single TPU/GPU without CPU-GPU data transfer bottlenecks.
* **Ecological Self-Balancing:** In ALife simulations, hardcoded curricula can be replaced by Lotka-Volterra dynamics (energy economics, regenerating food/seeds). This naturally induces cooperation, trade, and stigmergic communication out of raw survival pressure, all computed instantly in JAX matrices.

## 5. Conclusion: Path to AGI

The synthesis of these domains reveals a clear trajectory for AGI research:
1.  **Foundation:** Highly optimized, hardware-accelerated environments (JAX/JaxMARL) simulating multi-agent physics and economies at extreme speeds.
2.  **Cognition:** Agents trained via Evolutionary Strategies and Actor-Critic methods, utilizing Successor Features for zero-shot generalization and compositional reasoning across varying reward landscapes.
3.  **Communication:** The emergence of stigmergic, persistent communication driven by information asymmetry, bypassing the fragility of "cheap talk."
4.  **Agentic LLMs:** Integration of long-horizon optimization models capable of self-critique, tool-use (Constitutional AI / DPO), and iterative structural refinement over thousands of execution steps.
5.  **Adaptation:** Seamless transition to new paradigms via Warm-Starting and the Shrink-Perturb trick, ensuring models dynamically adapt without catastrophic forgetting or the need for perpetual cold-starts.
